# Writing style

From the *Frequentis Brand Guidelines Q4/2025*, pages 20–26 (page numbers are PDF page indexes). Applies to English text in any deliverable. German text: apply only the name rules, "Mio" for millions and the classification footer; do not rewrite German wording for style. Other languages: visual rules only.

## Language and case

- **British English** spelling and grammar: organisation, colour, centre, programme, defence, analyse, behaviour (p.20–21). US English only for material meant only for the US, with US terms (NextGen, 9-1-1, Defense) (p.21, p.40).
- **Sentence case** for headlines, sub-headlines and text; no random capitals (p.21). Expanding an abbreviation does not capitalise a common noun: "digital scanning (DS)", but "British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC)".
- Avoid bold; hierarchy comes from size (p.8, p.22). Left-align (p.22).

## Company and unit names

| Write | Not | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Frequentis | FREQUENTIS, FRQ | Sentence case in running text **and in slide headlines**; capitals only in print headlines (p.25) |
| Frequentis AG | Frequentis Ltd., FRQ AG | The legal entity (p.25) |
| Frequentis' portfolio | Frequentis's portfolio | Possessive (p.22) |
| Frequentis ATM offers … | ATM by FRQ | Business units in running text (p.25) |
| — | FRQ | Internal abbreviation only; never in external material (p.25) |

Colour-highlighting "Frequentis" in a headline: blue or grey only (p.25).

## Punctuation

- Colon: no space before, one after, no capital after it. The sentence that introduces a list ends with a colon; each bullet starts with a capital; a full stop only after the last bullet (p.21).
- Exclamation marks: sparingly, never more than one (p.22).
- "and", not "&", unless space truly forces it (p.22).
- "communications" alone or at the end of a compound ("mission-critical communications"); "communication" before a noun ("communication solutions") (p.22).

## Numbers, dates, times (p.22)

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| One to twelve in words, and any number that starts a sentence | five benefits; Twenty controllers… |
| Hyphenate compound numbers in words | twenty-three |
| Fractions in words, except in tables | two thirds |
| Decimal point; commas from four digits | 15.50; 2,115; €2,417,592.21 |
| Millions and billions | 10m, 15bn ("Mio" only in German) |
| Changes | +10%, €-4.3m |
| Dates | 25 March 2026 (no "th", no comma) |
| Times | 12:30 |

## Tone (p.23) and register (p.24)

- Shorter is better: most text can lose 30%. One idea per sentence; if it needs reading twice, rewrite it.
- Lead with the story: the headline carries the key message.
- Write for the reader; avoid jargon or explain it.
- Active voice, present tense, first-person plural "we", but not "At Frequentis we…". Be bold and precise.
- **Formal** (press, web, customer material): no contractions, colloquialisms, clichés, abbreviated words ("photograph", not "photo") or imperatives; spell out acronyms at first use. **Casual** (social, intranet) may relax these.
- Conflict C6: p.24 also says "third person, passive voice" for formal text, against p.23. This skill keeps active voice and "we" (as the earlier internal skill did) until Group Communications and Marketing confirms.

## Classification and copyright (p.26)

- Every non-public document: **Frequentis Public** (for public use), **Frequentis General** (Frequentis Inner Circle) or **Frequentis Confidential** (a specific group of employees). Ask the person; never guess.
- "© Frequentis AG <year>" with the current year; print adds "all rights reserved" and the standard disclaimer.
- Deck footer: `Frequentis <class> | © Frequentis AG <year>`, set in the slide master; for internal decks the author goes in the title-slide sub-title.
